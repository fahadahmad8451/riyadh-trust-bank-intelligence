# KYC Policy — Know Your Customer

## 1. Purpose

This Know Your Customer (KYC) Policy explains how Riyadh Trust Bank verifies the identity of every customer before opening an account or providing a banking service. The purpose of KYC is to prevent identity fraud, money laundering and terrorist financing, and to make sure the bank knows who it is doing business with. Every employee who opens accounts, updates customer records or reviews customer files must follow this policy.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all individual customers, joint account holders, minors with a guardian, and authorised representatives acting for a customer. It applies in every branch and through every digital channel of the bank. Corporate customers are covered by a separate Corporate KYC Procedure and are not covered here.

## 3. Documents Needed for Account Opening

To open a personal account, the customer must provide the following documents. No account may be opened until all required documents have been received and verified.

### 3.1 Identity documents

The customer must provide one valid original identity document. For Saudi nationals, this is a valid National ID card (Hawiya). For residents, this is a valid Iqama (residence permit). The document must not be expired. A photocopy alone is not accepted; the original must be seen by the branch officer and a certified copy kept on file.

### 3.2 Proof of address

The customer must provide proof of address dated within the last three months. Accepted proof includes a utility bill (electricity, water or telephone), a rental contract registered with the landlord, or a national address certificate. The address on the proof must match the address given on the account opening form.

### 3.3 Employment and income proof

For a salary account, the customer must provide an employment letter from the employer, or a salary certificate, showing the employer name, job title and monthly salary. For self-employed customers, a commercial registration and a recent bank statement of six months are required instead.

### 3.4 Additional documents for special cases

A guardian opening an account for a minor must provide the minor's birth certificate and the guardian's own identity document. An authorised representative must provide a valid power of attorney, stamped by a notary, plus their own identity document. Students must provide a valid student ID from their university or school.

## 4. Identity Verification Steps

The branch officer must complete the following steps in order. First, check that the identity document is original, undamaged and not expired. Second, compare the photograph on the document with the person in front of them. Third, enter the customer details into the core banking system and run the name against the internal watchlist and the sanctions list. Fourth, verify the proof of address and employment documents. Fifth, record the verification result and the officer's name in the system. If any step fails, the account must not be opened and the case must be sent to the Compliance Officer.

## 5. Customer Risk Rating

Every customer is given a risk rating after verification: Low, Medium or High. Most salaried customers with a clear employment history are Low risk. Customers with complex income sources, frequent large cash deposits or links to high-risk countries are Medium or High risk. High-risk customers require approval from the Compliance Officer before the account is opened, and their accounts are reviewed every six months instead of every year.

## 6. Ongoing Monitoring and Updates

KYC is not a one-time check. Customer information must be updated when the identity document expires, when the customer changes address or employment, or at least once every three years for Low-risk customers and once a year for Medium-risk customers. If a customer's documents expire and are not renewed within 30 days, the account is restricted until the documents are updated. The bank may ask for fresh documents at any time if a transaction looks unusual.

## 7. Record Keeping

All KYC documents, verification notes and risk ratings must be kept for at least ten years after the account is closed. Records must be stored securely, with access limited to authorised staff only. No KYC document may be shared outside the bank without the written approval of the Compliance Officer.

## 8. Responsibilities

Branch officers are responsible for collecting and checking documents. The Compliance Officer is responsible for approving High-risk customers and for training staff. The Branch Manager is responsible for making sure no account is opened without completed KYC. Any employee who finds a forged document must report it to the Compliance Officer on the same day.
