## 1. Purpose

This document summarises, in plain language, the main compliance duties that Riyadh Trust Bank follows under the rules of the Saudi Central Bank (SAMA). It is a training summary for bank staff. It is not the official SAMA rulebook and it does not replace the official regulations. Whenever this summary and an official SAMA circular disagree, the official circular must be followed. Staff who need the full text must ask the Compliance Department.

## 2. Who is SAMA

The Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) is the central bank of Saudi Arabia. It supervises banks, finance companies and insurance companies in the Kingdom. SAMA issues licences, sets prudential rules, and checks that banks follow them. SAMA can inspect a bank at any time, ask for reports, and take action against a bank that breaks the rules. Compliance with SAMA rules is therefore not optional.

## 3. Key SAMA Requirements

### 3.1 Licensing and governance

The bank must hold a valid SAMA banking licence and must have a board of directors, an audit committee and a risk committee. Senior managers must be fit and proper, which means they must have the right experience and a clean record. Any change in senior management must be reported to SAMA.

### 3.2 Capital and liquidity

The bank must hold enough capital to absorb losses, as set by SAMA's capital adequacy rules. It must also hold enough liquid assets to pay customers who withdraw their money. The Finance Department reports capital and liquidity figures to SAMA every month.

### 3.3 Customer protection

SAMA's Banking Consumer Protection Principles require the bank to treat customers fairly, to explain products and fees in clear language before the customer signs, to handle complaints quickly, and to protect customer data. A customer complaint must be answered within 15 working days. If the customer is not satisfied, they may complain to SAMA.

### 3.4 Cyber security framework

Under the SAMA Cyber Security Framework, the bank must protect its systems against cyber attacks, control who can access sensitive systems, monitor for unusual activity, and report serious cyber incidents to SAMA within the required time. Staff must not share passwords, must lock their screens, and must report phishing emails to the IT security team.

### 3.5 Anti-money laundering

SAMA requires banks to have an anti-money laundering programme, to verify customer identity, to monitor transactions, and to report suspicious activity. The details are in the bank's AML Policy. SAMA inspectors check these controls during their visits.

### 3.6 Reporting to SAMA

The bank must send regular reports to SAMA, including financial statements, large exposure reports, and reports of fraud. Reports must be accurate and sent on time. Late or wrong reporting is itself a breach of SAMA rules.

## 4. Compliance Calendar

The Compliance Department keeps a calendar of all SAMA reporting deadlines and inspection dates. Branch staff must send any information the Compliance Department asks for at least five working days before a deadline. Missing a deadline can lead to a fine, so no request from Compliance may be treated as low priority.

## 5. What Happens if the Bank Breaks the Rules

SAMA can warn the bank, fine the bank, restrict its activities, remove a manager, or in extreme cases withdraw the licence. Individual employees who deliberately break the rules can also be fined or banned from working in banking. For this reason, every employee must ask the Compliance Officer whenever they are unsure about a rule.
